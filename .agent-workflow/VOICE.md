# Just Home Leads voice brief (v3 "Straight Talk")

## Who is reading
A nervous first-time homebuyer in Dallas-Fort Worth. Often renting, often the first in their family to buy, often on a phone. Knows almost none of the industry words. Many are 25-40, some are 55+. They are smart, just new to this. They leave the moment a page feels like a textbook, a sales pitch, or a course.

## How we sound
A sharp friend who works in the business and tells you the truth over coffee. Warm, plain, confident, a little funny. Never condescending, never hype.

## Rules
1. Headline (H1) = the buyer's real question or worry in their own words, or a short, confident answer to it. 2 to 9 words. Examples of the right energy (these already exist on the site):
   - "Don't Hire Your Cousin. Unless She's a Shark."
   - "The 'Scary' Day Is Actually Boring"
   - "No W-2? No Problem. (Okay, Slight Problem.)"
   - "You Are The Employer. They Are The Employee."
   Other shapes: "How much house should you actually buy?", "What closing really costs in DFW", "Your credit, fixed in 90 days".
   Use sentence case or title case consistently with the page (title case is fine for H1).
2. Hero subtitle = 1 to 2 short sentences answering "what do I get here and why should I care?" Plain words. Max ~30 words.
3. Intro paragraph (the one under "In this guide") = 2 to 4 short sentences. Say the reassuring truth first, then what the page will help them do. No throat-clearing ("In today's market...", "Buying a home is one of the biggest decisions...").
4. Intro objectives (the checklist items under "In this guide") = each starts with a verb, under ~10 words, plain: "Figure out how much you need to save", not "Understand down payment requirements and optimization strategies".
5. Industry terms: say it plainly first, then the term in parentheses the first time: "the fee you pay to hold the house (earnest money)". Never stack 3 undefined terms in one sentence. Acronyms spelled out on first use (DTI, PMI, ISD, DPA, HOA...).
6. No em dashes anywhere (use commas, periods, colons, or "to"). No emoji. No "No BS" (it is overused on the site already). No hype words: "ultimate", "master", "masterclass", "like a pro", "comprehensive", "unlock", "journey" (as filler), "empower", "leverage" (as a verb), "game-changer".
7. Do NOT invent facts, numbers, statistics, testimonials, or claims. Only reuse numbers already on the page (or, when merging two pages, the more conservative / more widely true one). If you are unsure a number is right, leave it out of the intro.
8. Do not use "student", "course", "lesson", "module", "phase", "stage" framing.
9. Texas specifics are a strength: keep them (option period, homestead exemption, ISDs, DFW counties) but explain them.
10. Keep the page's HTML structure, classes, ids, data-* attributes and scripts intact. Only change text nodes. Keep the <title> and meta description aligned with the new H1 (title under ~60 chars, ending " | Just Home Leads"; description 140-160 chars, plain). Also update og:title / og:description to match.
